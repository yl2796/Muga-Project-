About the Project

My engagement with this project began when I decided to pursue a field experience/internship focused on digital archives and metadata. I reached out to Kayo Denda, Head of the Margery Somers Foster Center and Librarian for Women’s, Gender, and Sexuality Studies at the Mabel Smith Douglass Library.

Following our conversation, I saw an exciting opportunity to design my own practicum—one that integrated my existing coursework with my growing interests in digital archives, metadata, and programming. At the same time, I wanted to anchor my work in women’s, gender, and sexuality studies, particularly focusing on gender dynamics and societal structures in Korea.

I chose to work with Korean Shamanic songs (muga), inspired partly by my mother’s lifelong appreciation for muga performances. This project provided a meaningful avenue to explore my own cultural heritage while examining historical primary sources. Specifically, I focused on muga from the late Joseon Dynasty (17th to 19th centuries)—a period when Neo-Confucianism deeply reshaped Korean society and traditional gender roles—to analyze how these cultural transformations are reflected in the texts.
