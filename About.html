About the Project

I started to get engaged in the project because, at the time I decided to do a field experience/internship, I was interested in digital archives and metadata. So, I decided to contact Kayo Dena, the head of the Margery Somers Foster Center and Librarian for Women’s, Gender, and Sexuality Studies at the Mabel Smith Douglass Library. After the conversation I had with her, I thought it would be interesting to design my own practicum, combining my interests with what I learned from the previous courses I took. By the time I talked to Kayo, I was interested in digital archives, metadata, and programming, and I wanted to develop more skills in these areas. I thought it was a great opportunity. Still, I wanted to cover the topic of women’s, gender, and sexuality, as this was also a topic I was interested in. I recalled being interested in feminism in Korean society and oppression within that society. I decided to work with a Korean Shamanic song (muga) because I remembered my mother was interested in muga and how she enjoyed watching performances multiple times. At the same time, I thought it was a great opportunity for me to learn about my own culture and historical background regarding this topic. I thought it would be interesting to learn about the muga from the late Joseon Dynasty, especially from the 17th to 19th centuries, because that is when Neo-Confucianism impacted society and gender roles within it. I wanted to see how they are reflected in these texts.
